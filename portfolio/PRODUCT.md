# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Hiring managers and technical leads at AEC firms — WSP, Arup, Mott MacDonald and similar infrastructure consultancies. They are reviewing candidates for Digital Delivery, BIM Management, or Automation roles. They arrive from a CV or LinkedIn link, spend 60–90 seconds forming an impression, and are looking for evidence of technical credibility, real project delivery, and someone who communicates clearly. Secondary audience: direct contacts who have already met Karthik and are verifying his depth.

## Product Purpose

A personal portfolio for Karthik Jyothy, a BIM and Digital Delivery professional based in Belfast. The site exists to convert a CV review into a confident hiring decision. Success looks like: the visitor closes the tab believing Karthik is technically serious, has solved real problems on real projects, and would be an asset to a digital delivery team at a major infrastructure firm.

## Brand Personality

Warm, expert, approachable. Technical authority without coldness. The voice of a senior engineer who explains things clearly and has done the work — not a marketer, not a hacker, not a generalist.

Three words: **precise, grounded, human**.

## Anti-references

- Generic developer portfolio: dark backgrounds, neon accents, matrix/code aesthetic. This is infrastructure, not software.
- Startup SaaS landing page: "supercharge your workflow", buzzword-heavy copy, conversion-optimised pressure. This is a professional introduction, not a sales funnel.
- Plain resume dump: a web version of a Word document with no visual identity or considered hierarchy.

## Design Principles

1. **Evidence over assertion.** Every claim should be backed by a specific outcome, project, or tool. No adjectives without nouns.
2. **Clarity is the luxury.** The most impressive thing this portfolio can do is be immediately legible. No hierarchy-hunting, no ambiguity about what to read next.
3. **Technical confidence, not technical decoration.** The design should feel like it belongs in an AEC firm's internal deck — precise, considered, no gratuitous ornament.
4. **Warmth at the margins.** Typography, spacing, and copy should feel like a person wrote and built this, not an AI template. Small choices (line breaks, caption tone, micro-copy) carry this.
5. **Mobile is a hiring context too.** Hiring managers review CVs on phones. Every section must work at 375px without loss of credibility.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA as a baseline. 4.5:1 contrast for body text, 3:1 for large/bold text. Full keyboard navigation. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` — animations should degrade gracefully to instant transitions. No colour-only information encoding.
